About this tracker

This dashboard analyzes European patents (EPO) between 1990 and 2024 that cite scientific publications identified as artificial intelligence, as well as the main players (firms, inventors and publications) at the heart of this dynamic.

Identifying AI patents

A patent is classified as an AI patent using two complementary methods:

  • Observed : the patent cites at least one publication whose primary topic in OpenAlex is one of 34 AI topics. The link is made through patCit (EPO), through the DOI written in the citation, or by finding the cited title in arXiv, Crossref or Semantic Scholar.
  • Predicted : for NPL citations that could not be linked to any publication, an ML model classifies the title of the cited publication as AI or not (threshold chosen to reproduce the number of AI citations observed on recent patents). The patent is predicted AI if at least one of its citations is classified as AI. The model first serves to flag the citations to check: those it deems possibly AI are searched for in bibliographic databases, and only those that cannot be found keep its prediction.

Counting

  • One patent = one EP application. The EPO often publishes the same patent several times (A1, A2, A3, B1…); these publications are grouped and counted only once, in the year of the first publication.
  • Geography (countries, regions) uses REGPAT fractional weighting; each patent weighs 1 in total.
  • Scientific publications (AI publications and Key players tabs) can only be identified for observed citations.

Detailed method: methodological note Artificial intelligence in the service of innovation (Bergeaud, Delbouve, Terra, 2026).
